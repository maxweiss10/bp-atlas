## ROW 1 — ACS — **CORRECTED (mostly confirmed, two real fixes + one required addition)**

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| **BP target** | SBP <140 mmHg within the first hour. **CONFIRMED** — 2025 AHA/ACC HTN guideline, Class 1, LOE C-LD: for a hypertensive emergency with a compelling condition, reduce SBP "<140 mm Hg for most conditions and to <120 mm Hg in aortic dissection during the first hour." |
| **"keep DBP >60"** | **Not a guideline recommendation.** No ACC/AHA document sets a DBP floor. It is physiologic/expert consensus: the LV is perfused in diastole, coronary perfusion pressure ≈ DBP − LVEDP, and coronary autoregulation fails below a CPP of ~60 mmHg. Backed by observational J-curve data (DBP <60 associated with worse outcomes after MI). **Keep it, but label it expert consensus, not guideline.** |
| **Preferred agents** | IV/topical nitroglycerin; esmolol or labetalol; nicardipine or clevidipine. |
| **"esmolol > labetalol"** | **Not a guideline preference** — the 2025 HTN guideline lists esmolol and labetalol together. The rationale is pharmacokinetic, not hierarchical: esmolol is β1-selective, onset <1 min, t½ ~9 min, effect gone 10-30 min after stop, so it can be turned off if HF or shock declares itself. Reframe as "esmolol if tolerance to beta blockade is uncertain." |
| **Also worth adding** | The 2025 ACS guideline recommends **oral** beta blocker within 24 h (Class 1). Routine **IV** beta blockade in ACS is not recommended — the card's drip-centric framing should say so. |
| **AVOID — nitroprusside** | **CONFIRMED.** Coronary steal: SNP dilates resistance arterioles and redistributes flow away from ischemic myocardium. Cohn (NEJM 1982) randomized trial — SNP started <9 h from MI onset increased mortality; benefit only when started later. Also boxed warning for cyanide toxicity. |
| **AVOID — hydralazine** | **CONFIRMED as expert opinion** (no Class 3 statement exists). Reason: unpredictable magnitude and long duration of effect plus reflex tachycardia → increased myocardial O2 demand. |
| **AVOID — beta blocker if LV failure w/ pulm edema, HR <60, SBP <100, shock, high-grade heart block** | **CONFIRMED.** 2025 ACS guideline: avoid/discontinue beta blockers with signs of HF, low-output state, or risk of cardiogenic shock. Evidence = **COMMIT/CCS-2** (n=45,852; early IV then oral metoprolol → no reduction in death/reinfarction/arrest but **increased cardiogenic shock**, risk highest on day 1, concentrated in Killip II-III, hypotensive and elderly patients). **ADD: active bronchospasm / severe reactive airway disease.** |
| **Nitrates in RV infarction** | Card says "judicious nitro w/ RVMI." **Guideline language is stronger — avoid.** 2025 ACS guideline: avoid nitroglycerin with suspected RV infarction, SBP <90 mmHg, or a fall in SBP >30 mmHg below baseline. Recommend changing "judicious" → "avoid." |
| **OMITTED — PDE5 inhibitors** | **MUST ADD.** 2025 ACS guideline: avoid nitrates within **12 h of avanafil, 24 h of sildenafil or vardenafil, 48 h of tadalafil.** (The card's omission of this is the single most clinically dangerous gap in the row.) |

**Citations:** 2025 ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI ACS Guideline, Circulation 2025, doi 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309 — https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309 · https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2024.11.009 · Summary: https://www.guidelinecentral.com/guideline/4374545/ · 2025 AHA/ACC HTN Guideline (above) · COMMIT: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16271643/ · SNP in MI: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM198205133061901

**Verdict: CONFIRMED with corrections.** Fix "judicious nitro w/ RVMI" → "avoid"; add PDE5i intervals; de-rank "esmolol > labetalol"; add bronchospasm to the no-BB list; footnote DBP >60 as expert consensus.

## ROW 2 — ACUTE PULMONARY EDEMA / ACUTE HF — **CONFIRMED with important additions**

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| **BP target** | SBP <140 within 1 h. **CONFIRMED** (2025 AHA/ACC HTN guideline compelling-condition target). |
| **Nitroglycerin** | **CONFIRMED and should be promoted to front-line.** 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA HF guideline Class 2b, B-NR: "In patients with ADHF and dyspnea, IV nitroglycerin or nitroprusside may be considered as an adjunct to diuretic therapy for relief of dyspnea." In the specific hypertensive phenotype (SCAPE — sympathetic crashing acute pulmonary edema), **high-dose nitroglycerin** is the front-line agent: bolus ~400–2000 mcg over ~2 min then a high-rate infusion (100–300+ mcg/min), titrated down as it resolves. The card's Table 3 max of 400 mcg/min understates practice in SCAPE. |
| **Is nitroprusside really recommended here?** | **YES — CONFIRMED,** but only at Class 2b, B-NR and only as an adjunct. Caveats to carry: requires arterial line; cyanide/thiocyanate accumulation with renal or hepatic impairment, high dose or prolonged use; avoid if concomitant ACS/CAD (steal). |
| **Are beta blockers "not preferred" or contraindicated?** | **"Not preferred" is too soft in one direction and too harsh in the other — split the statement.** (a) **IV beta blockers are contraindicated** in acute decompensation with hypotension, low output or cardiogenic shock. (b) **Chronic oral beta blockers should be CONTINUED**, not stopped, unless the patient is hypotensive/low-output; new initiation is deferred until euvolemic and off IV inotropes/vasodilators. |
| **OMITTED — non-invasive ventilation** | **MUST ADD.** Early NIPPV (CPAP or BiPAP) is a core therapy in hypertensive acute pulmonary edema — reduces preload/afterload, recruits alveoli, reduces intubation rate. |
| **OMITTED — loop diuretic nuance** | **MUST ADD.** IV loop diuretic is standard in volume-overloaded ADHF, but in SCAPE most patients are **volume-redistributed, not volume-overloaded** — diuretics are not the primary therapy and should not delay vasodilation + NIPPV. |
| **AVOID** | IV beta blockade in decompensation; nitroprusside if concurrent ACS or renal failure; nitrates within the PDE5i windows above. |

**Citations:** 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA HF Guideline, Circulation 2022;145:e895, doi 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001063 — https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001063 · SCAPE reviews: https://www.thepermanentejournal.org/doi/10.7812/TPP/23.149 · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0735675724007447
*(No newer US HF guideline exists; the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA document is still current. ESC published a 2026 HF guideline but it does not change this row for US practice.)*

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** Add NIPPV, the "diuretic is not primary in SCAPE" nuance, high-dose NTG, and split the beta-blocker statement into IV vs chronic oral.

## ROW 3 — AORTIC DISSECTION — **CORRECTED (heart rate number is wrong)**

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| **BP target** | SBP <120 mmHg, **or the lowest BP that maintains end-organ perfusion.** **CONFIRMED** — 2022 ACC/AHA Aortic Disease Guideline, Class 1. Corroborated by 2025 AHA/ACC HTN guideline (Class 1, C-LD: "<120 mm Hg in aortic dissection during the first hour"). **The "or lowest BP maintaining end-organ perfusion" clause is missing from the card and matters at the bedside.** |
| **HR target — "HR <60"** | **CORRECTED → HR 60–80 bpm.** The 2022 ACC/AHA aortic guideline target is **60–80 bpm**. "<60" is the **2010** guideline's target and is now superseded. |
| **Time frame — "within 20 min"** | Not from the 2022 aortic guideline (which gives no time window). It comes from the hypertensive-emergency literature / 2024 AHA acute-care statement ("SBP ≤120 in ~20 minutes"). Fine to keep; attribute to the acute-care statement, not the aortic guideline. |
| **SEQUENCING — beta blocker BEFORE vasodilator** | **CONFIRMED and it is the single most important rule in the row.** 2022 guideline, Class 1: "Unless contraindicated, IV beta blockers are recommended as initial management, and vasodilators if BP is not adequately controlled with beta-blockers." **Rationale to put on the card:** a vasodilator given first causes reflex tachycardia and increased inotropy, raising dP/dt (rate of rise of aortic pressure) and promoting propagation of the dissection. Aortic wall stress is a function of dP/dt, not BP alone — which is why rate control is not optional. |
| **If beta blockade is contraindicated** | (severe bronchospasm, high-grade AV block, acute decompensated HF) → **non-dihydropyridine CCB: IV diltiazem or verapamil** for rate control. Less-established alternative but the accepted fallback. **Special case: with significant acute aortic regurgitation, do not aggressively lower heart rate** — bradycardia lengthens diastole and worsens the regurgitant volume. |
| **Preferred agents** | IV esmolol or labetalol first (esmolol most titratable); then nicardipine or clevidipine. Nitroprusside is an acceptable second agent here but adds cyanide risk. **Add IV opioid analgesia** — pain control lowers sympathetic drive and is part of standard management. |
| **AVOID** | Any vasodilator as the first agent (reflex tachycardia, ↑dP/dt). Hydralazine (unpredictable, reflex tachycardia, raises wall shear stress). |

**Citations:** 2022 ACC/AHA Aortic Disease Guideline, Circulation 2022;146:e334, PMID 36322642 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36322642/ · ACC Ten Points Part 2: https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/ten-points-to-remember/2022/11/01/12/21/2022-guideline-on-aortic-disease-2-gl-ad

**Verdict: CORRECTED.** "HR <60" → "HR 60–80." Add the end-organ-perfusion clause, the dP/dt rationale, and the non-DHP CCB fallback.

## ROW 4 — ISCHEMIC STROKE — **CORRECTED (threshold misread as a target; two Class 3 recs missing)**

Now governed by the **2026 AHA/ASA AIS guideline**, which replaces the 2018/2019 documents.

| Scenario | Recommendation | COR / LOE |
|---|---|---|
| Before IV thrombolysis | SBP <185 and DBP <110 before starting IVT | 1, B-NR — **CONFIRMED** |
| **First 24 h after IVT** | **<180/105 for at least the first 24 h** | 1, B-R — **CARD OMITS THIS. MUST ADD.** |
| Intensive lowering after IVT | Targeting SBP <140 after IVT → **no benefit** | **3: No Benefit, B-R — NEW, card omits** |
| Before EVT when no IVT given | ≤185/110 | 2a, B-NR — card omits |
| During and 24 h after EVT | ≤180/105 | 2a, B-NR — card omits |
| **After successful reperfusion (mTICI 2b/2c/3)** | Targeting **SBP <140 in the first 24–72 h is HARMFUL** | **3: Harm, LOE A — NEW, card omits. Evidence: ENCHANTED2/MT and OPTIMAL-BP (both showed worse 90-day function with intensive lowering); BEST-II supportive.** |
| No reperfusion therapy, **BP <220/120**, no comorbid indication | Initiating/reinitiating antihypertensives in the first 48–72 h is **not effective** | **3: No Benefit, LOE A** |
| No reperfusion therapy, **BP ≥220/120** | Benefit uncertain; **might be reasonable to lower by ~15% in the first 24 h** | 2b, C-LD |
| Comorbid condition demanding BP reduction | Treat regardless of the above — concomitant ACS, acute HF, aortic dissection, post-fibrinolysis sICH, pre-eclampsia/eclampsia | 1, C-EO |

**The card's error:** "<220/120 if no lysis" reads as a *target*. It is a **treatment threshold** — below 220/120 you do nothing; at or above it you may lower by ~15%, and even then only "might be reasonable." Suggested card wording: **"No lysis/EVT: treat only if ≥220/120, then lower ~15% over 24 h (permissive HTN)."**

**Agents:** labetalol, nicardipine, clevidipine (carried forward from the 2019 Table 5); hydralazine or enalaprilat "may also be considered"; **nitroprusside if BP remains uncontrolled or DBP >140.**
> **Cross-check for the card's Table 3:** it lists nitroprusside as "avoid in ... CVA." The stroke guideline actually names nitroprusside as the fallback for refractory BP. The caution is defensible on ICP grounds (cerebral vasodilation) but should not be stated as a guideline prohibition.

**Citations:** 2026 AHA/ASA AIS Guideline, Stroke 2026, doi 10.1161/STR.0000000000000513 — https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/STR.0000000000000513 · https://www.guidelinecentral.com/guideline/7091/ · 2019 predecessor Table 5 (agents/doses): https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/STR.0000000000000211 · ENCHANTED2/MT: https://www.tctmd.com/news/extreme-bp-lowering-harmful-after-stroke-thrombectomy-enchanted2mt · OPTIMAL-BP: https://www.tctmd.com/news/optimal-bp-shows-harm-too-much-bp-lowering-after-stroke-thrombectomy

**Verdict: CORRECTED.** Add post-IVT <180/105 × 24 h; add the post-EVT harm rule; reframe 220/120 as a threshold.

## ROW 5 — HEMORRHAGIC STROKE (ICH) — **CORRECTED — this is the most out-of-date row on the card**

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Card says** | "SBP >220, reduce by 25%. If SBP 180–220, target reduction to 140–160." |
| **Current — 2025 AHA/ACC HTN guideline** | **Class 2a, LOE A:** for acute spontaneous ICH presenting with **SBP 150–220**, "immediately lower SBP to **130 to <140 mm Hg for at least 7 days**... but **stop antihypertensive medications if SBP <130**." |
| **2022 AHA/ASA ICH guideline** | SBP 150–220, mild-to-moderate ICH: lower to a target of **140**, maintaining **130–150** (Class 2b, B-R). Start treatment **within 2 h of onset and reach target within 1 h** (Class 2a, C-LD). **Smooth, sustained control avoiding peaks and large SBP variability** is beneficial (Class 2a) — an outcome-relevant point the card omits entirely. |
| **Is 140–160 right?** | **No — it is too high at both ends and the entry band is wrong.** Entry band should be **150–220**, not 180–220. Target should be **~130–140**, not 140–160. |
| **Is there a floor?** | **Yes. SBP <130 is Class 3: HARM (B-R).** Card omits this. |
| **"SBP >220 → reduce by 25%"** | **Not supported by either current guideline.** These patients were largely excluded from INTERACT2/ATACH-2 so the evidence is thin. The operative rule in the 2025 guideline is that SBP should not be lowered below 130. Suggested card wording: **"SBP >220: limited evidence; continuous titratable infusion with arterial line; do not drop below 130."** |
| **Caution about large/rapid drops** | Over-rapid or excessive lowering → reduced cerebral perfusion pressure and **acute kidney injury** (the ATACH-2 renal signal at 7 days). Hence the emphasis on smooth titration over bolus dosing. |
| **Trial evidence** | **INTERACT2** (n=2,839; <140 vs <180): primary outcome of death/major disability NS, but the ordinal shift analysis favored intensive lowering. **ATACH-2** (n=1,000; 110–139 vs 140–179): no benefit, **more renal adverse events**. **INTERACT3** (n=7,036, stepped-wedge; care bundle with SBP target <140 plus glucose, temperature, anticoagulation reversal): **improved functional outcome** — supports the bundle, not BP alone. **INTERACT4** (ambulance-delivered ultra-early lowering): benefit in ICH, potential harm in ischemic stroke — which is the reason you do not lower BP before the CT. |
| **Agents** | Nicardipine or clevidipine infusions (most reliable smooth control); labetalol. Arterial line when feasible. |

**Citations:** 2025 AHA/ACC HTN Guideline (COR 2a, LOE A) — https://www.guidelinecentral.com/guideline/6962/ · 2022 AHA/ASA ICH Guideline, Stroke 2022;53:e282, doi 10.1161/STR.0000000000000407 — https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/STR.0000000000000407 · emDocs recap w/ COR/LOE: https://www.emdocs.net/intracerebral-hemorrhage-2022-guideline-update/ · INTERACT3: https://www.thelancet.com/article/S0140-6736(23)00806-1/fulltext

**Verdict: CORRECTED — rewrite the row.** Proposed: *"SBP 150–220: lower to 130–<140 within 1 h (start within 2 h of onset) and maintain ≥7 days; hold if SBP <130 (harm). SBP >220: limited evidence, titrate smoothly, do not go below 130. Avoid peaks and variability — infusion, not boluses."*

## ROW 6 — PRES — **CONFIRMED as expert consensus only; label it**

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Card says** | "Immediate MAP decline 20–25%." |
| **Actual evidence/source** | **Expert consensus. There is no RCT and no dedicated society guideline for PRES.** The 20–25% figure is a carry-over of the general hypertensive-emergency principle (MAP reduction of 20–25% in the first hour) applied to PRES by reviews and case series. Reviews consistently state that no consensus method exists and that early antihypertensive approaches in the literature are "diverse, even contradictory." **Recommend the card annotate it "(expert consensus, no RCT)."** |
| **Refinement worth adding** | Lower MAP by ~20–25% over the **first 1–2 h**; a MAP of ~105–125 mmHg is a commonly used landing zone, individualized to the patient's baseline. |
| **Why not more** | PRES overlaps with **RCVS**; excessive or rapid lowering can precipitate watershed ischemia/infarction. |
| **Preferred agents** | Titratable infusion — **nicardipine or clevidipine**; labetalol as an adjunct. |
| **AVOID** | **Nitroglycerin and nitroprusside** — cerebral vasodilation can raise ICP and worsen edema; nitroprusside additionally carries cyanide risk. (Card's generic "same agents as stroke" is acceptable but the nitrate caution is worth one clause.) |
| **Non-BP actions the card omits** | **Remove the trigger** — calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, cyclosporine), VEGF inhibitors, cytotoxic chemotherapy. Treat seizures. In a pregnant/postpartum patient, PRES **is** the eclampsia pathway → magnesium sulfate + the obstetric algorithm below. |
| **Hypertensive encephalopathy vs PRES** | **Overlapping, not distinct.** PRES is a **radiographic** diagnosis (vasogenic edema, typically parieto-occipital); hypertensive encephalopathy is the **clinical** syndrome and can occur with a normal MRI. Same BP approach. **Recommend merging into one row: "Hypertensive encephalopathy / PRES."** Hypertensive encephalopathy is a diagnosis of exclusion — rule out ischemic stroke, ICH and SAH first; symptom reversal with BP lowering is confirmatory. |

**Citations:** https://emcrit.org/ibcc/pres/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3943026/ (hypertensive encephalopathy review) · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7596407/ (PRES + infarction, BP management challenges)

**Verdict: CONFIRMED (with the caveat that it is consensus, not evidence).** Merge with hypertensive encephalopathy; add the nitrate caution and "remove the trigger."

## ROW 7 — SAH — **MISSING ROW; ADD IT (and the nimodipine trap)**

The card lists nicardipine/clevidipine as "indicated for SAH" in Table 3 but gives SAH no row in Table 2. It needs one, because SAH is the condition where the answer is least like the others.

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| **BP target before the aneurysm is secured** | **There is no guideline-endorsed number.** 2023 AHA/ASA aSAH guideline states verbatim that "available evidence is **insufficient to recommend any specific BP target**." Numbers in circulation are **SBP <160** (most commonly cited; rebleeding meta-analyses show higher rates above SBP 160) or **<180**. The card should say this explicitly rather than inventing a number. |
| **What IS Class 1** | **Class 1, C-EO:** in aSAH with an unsecured aneurysm, **frequent BP monitoring and BP control with short-acting medication(s)** is recommended to avoid severe **hypotension**, severe **hypertension**, and **BP variability**. Note that hypotension and variability are named alongside hypertension — the goal is stability, not a low number. |
| **Explicit caution** | "**Sudden, profound reduction of BP should be avoided.**" Factors to weigh when choosing a target: presenting BP, brain swelling, hydrocephalus, history of hypertension and renal impairment. |
| **After the aneurysm is secured** | The logic **inverts** — the concern shifts from rebleeding to delayed cerebral ischemia, and elevated BP is *maintained* (Class 1: elevating BP is reasonable for DCI). Worth one clause on the card so the intern does not keep driving BP down post-coiling. |
| **Preferred agents** | Short-acting titratable: **nicardipine or clevidipine** infusion; labetalol boluses. (Card's Table 3 is already right on this.) |
| **NIMODIPINE — the trap** | **Class 1: enteral nimodipine 60 mg q4h × 21 days** is beneficial for preventing delayed cerebral ischemia and improving functional outcomes. **It is NOT a BP-lowering therapy and must not be counted toward the antihypertensive regimen — but it frequently causes hypotension.** If it does, the correct move is to **split the dose to 30 mg q2h**, not to discontinue it. Guideline also notes that interrupting nimodipine is associated with worse outcomes, and that induced hypertension can be maintained even in the setting of nimodipine-induced hypotension. |
| **Other Class 1 / Class 3 items** | Class 1: emergency **anticoagulation reversal**. Class 3 (No Benefit): **routine antifibrinolytic therapy** (ULTRA trial — no reduction in rebleeding, no functional benefit). Class 3: routine statin and IV magnesium not recommended. |

**Citations:** 2023 AHA/ASA aSAH Guideline, Stroke 2023;54:e314, doi 10.1161/STR.0000000000000436 — https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/STR.0000000000000436 · Full PDF verified: https://neurosciences.ucsd.edu/centers-programs/neurocritical-care/_files/national-guidelines/AHA%20SAH%20guideline%202023-guideline-for-the-management-of-patients-with-aneurysmal-subarachnoid-hemorrhage-a-guideline-from.pdf

**Verdict: ADD ROW.** Suggested: *"SAH (unsecured aneurysm): no evidence-based target — commonly SBP <160. Short-acting titratable agents (nicardipine/clevidipine); avoid hypotension and BP variability as much as hypertension; avoid sudden profound drops. Nimodipine 60 mg PO q4h × 21 d is for DCI, NOT for BP — if it drops the pressure, split to 30 mg q2h rather than stop. After securing: permissive/induced HTN for DCI."*

# CONDITIONS THE CARD OMITS — recommended additions

## A. PRE-ECLAMPSIA WITH SEVERE FEATURES / ECLAMPSIA — **highest-priority addition**

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Threshold** | **SBP ≥160 OR DBP ≥110, persistent for ≥15 min** (accurately measured) = hypertensive emergency. Applies **antepartum, intrapartum AND postpartum** — postpartum pre-eclampsia can present up to 6 weeks after delivery. |
| **Time window** | **Treat within 30–60 min of confirmed severe-range BP**, to reduce the risk of maternal stroke. Corroborated by 2025 AHA/ACC HTN guideline, **Class 1, LOE B-R**: confirm on repeat within 15 min, then lower to **<160/<110 within 30–60 min**. |
| **Target after treatment** | ACOG, verbatim: "The goal is **not to normalize BP**, but to achieve a range of **140–150/90–100 mm Hg**" — to avoid repeated prolonged exposure to severe systolic hypertension and loss of cerebral autoregulation. |
| **First-line agents (all three are first-line)** | **IV labetalol** 20 mg → 40 mg → 80 mg, each over ≥2 min, q10 min (cumulative max 300 mg). **IV hydralazine** 5 or 10 mg over ≥2 min → 10 mg q20 min. **PO immediate-release nifedipine** 10 mg → 20 mg → 20 mg q20 min. IR nifedipine is first-line particularly when IV access is unavailable; **capsules are swallowed, never punctured or given sublingually.** |
| **Escalation** | If three appropriate doses fail → emergent consultation with MFM, anesthesia, internal medicine or critical care for second-line therapy. |
| **MAGNESIUM SULFATE** | **ACOG, verbatim: "Magnesium sulfate is not recommended as an antihypertensive agent."** It is **seizure prophylaxis/treatment for eclampsia** (typically 4–6 g load over 20–30 min, then 1–2 g/h). It does not substitute for antihypertensive therapy. This is the single most common conceptual error on this topic. |
| **AVOID** | **ACEi, ARB, direct renin inhibitors, atenolol, nitroprusside, MRA — 2025 AHA/ACC Class 3: Harm, C-LD.** Labetalol: caution/avoid with asthma or maternal bradycardia (also neonatal bradycardia). Hydralazine: maternal hypotension. Nifedipine: maternal tachycardia, overshoot hypotension. |
| **Other** | Cardiac monitoring is **not** required for IV labetalol, IV hydralazine or IR nifedipine. Stabilize BP **before** delivery or transfer, even in urgent circumstances. Definitive treatment of pre-eclampsia is delivery. Blunt the hypertensive response to intubation if general anesthesia is needed. |

**Citations:** ACOG Committee Opinion 692, "Emergent Therapy for Acute-Onset, Severe Hypertension During Pregnancy and the Postpartum Period," Obstet Gynecol 2017 (full text verified) — https://ilpqc.org/wp-content/docs/htn/ACOG%20Committee%20Opinion%20692.pdf · ACOG Practice Bulletin 222, Gestational Hypertension and Preeclampsia (2020 interim update) — https://www.preeclampsia.org/frontend/assets/img/advocacy_resource/Gestational_Hypertension_and_Preeclampsia_ACOG_Practice_Bulletin,_Number_222_1605448006.pdf · 2025 AHA/ACC HTN Guideline §5.5 — https://www.guidelinecentral.com/guideline/6962/

## B. SYMPATHETIC / CATECHOLAMINE CRISIS — **high-priority addition**

Covers pheochromocytoma crisis, cocaine/methamphetamine, MAOI–tyramine reaction, clonidine withdrawal.

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Agent of choice** | **Phentolamine** — non-selective alpha blocker, IV 5 mg bolus, repeat q10 min or infusion. Directly reverses catecholamine-mediated vasoconstriction and coronary vasospasm. |
| **Also preferred** | **Nicardipine or clevidipine** (2025 AHA/ACC lists these ± phentolamine for catecholamine excess). **Benzodiazepines are first-line adjuncts** in cocaine/methamphetamine toxicity — they reduce central sympathetic outflow and often lower BP without a specific antihypertensive. Nitroglycerin or nitroprusside for refractory hypertension. |
| **Pheochromocytoma — the rule** | **Alpha blockade ALWAYS before beta blockade** (Endocrine Society). A beta blocker given first removes β2-mediated vasodilation and leaves alpha-mediated vasoconstriction unopposed → paradoxical, potentially fatal hypertensive crisis. |
| **Cocaine + beta blockers — what the evidence actually says** | The **AHA scientific statement on cocaine-associated chest pain (Circulation 2008)** says beta blockers should not be given acutely, because of concern for provoking/exacerbating coronary spasm (propranolol worsened cocaine-induced coronary vasoconstriction in the classic study). **However, the modern evidence does not support the harm.** Multiple systematic reviews/meta-analyses (2018–2024, including ~1,794 ED patients with cocaine-associated chest pain) found **no difference in in-hospital mortality or non-fatal MI** with beta blockers; **labetalol** (mixed α/β) has the strongest safety data and did not worsen coronary vasoconstriction after intranasal cocaine. A 2024 *Journal of Hospital Medicine* "Things We Do for No Reason" and a 2022 *CMAJ* practice article both argue that avoidance is not supported by good evidence. **Honest card framing: "Benzos + phentolamine/CCB first. The classic 'unopposed alpha' prohibition is guideline-level but weakly evidenced — do not reflexively stop a patient's chronic beta blocker for a positive cocaine screen."** |
| **Clonidine withdrawal** | The definitive fix is to **restart clonidine** (oral or transdermal). For the crisis itself: **phentolamine** or **labetalol**. Do **not** give a beta blocker alone — unopposed alpha. Prevention: taper clonidine over 2–4 days; if the patient is also on a beta blocker, **stop the beta blocker several days before starting the clonidine taper.** |
| **MAOI + tyramine (or sympathomimetic)** | **Phentolamine**; nicardipine/clevidipine. Avoid beta-blocker monotherapy. (Distinct from serotonin syndrome, which is the MAOI + serotonergic-agent problem.) |
| **AVOID across the group** | **Beta-blocker monotherapy** — 2025 AHA/ACC explicitly lists "avoid β-blocker monotherapy" for catecholamine excess. |

## C. AKI / MALIGNANT HYPERTENSION WITH RENAL TMA, AND SCLERODERMA RENAL CRISIS

**C1. Hypertensive emergency with AKI ± MAHA (malignant hypertension with renal thrombotic microangiopathy)**
- **Target:** gradual. A reasonable early landing zone is SBP ~160, then slower reduction. Over-rapid lowering worsens AKI (the ATACH-2 renal signal is the cleanest demonstration of this principle).
- **Preferred:** **nicardipine or clevidipine** — no renal dose adjustment, no cyanide/thiocyanate risk, smooth titration. Labetalol acceptable.
- **AVOID:** **nitroprusside** with renal impairment (thiocyanate accumulation). **ACEi/ARB acutely** — efferent arteriolar vasodilation drops filtration pressure and can worsen AKI, especially if volume-depleted. The card's MAHA mention under "end-organ damage" has no corresponding management row.

**C2. Scleroderma renal crisis — the one hypertensive emergency where an ACE inhibitor is the specific drug**
- **Agent: captopril**, not because it is a good antihypertensive but because SRC is renin-driven. Captopril is preferred over other ACEi because of the rapid onset (peak 60–90 min) and short duration, which permit rapid escalation.
- **Dosing:** start 6.25–12.5 mg, increase by 12.5–25 mg q4–8 h until baseline BP is reached.
- **Rate:** lower SBP by ~20 mmHg (or DBP ~10 mmHg) **per 24 h**, to baseline over ~72 h — deliberately slow.
- **Key teaching point:** **continue the ACEi even if creatinine rises or dialysis is required** — renal recovery can occur months later. Other agents (CCB, ARB) are **add-ons only**; ARBs are not equivalent. Avoid high-dose corticosteroids (>15 mg/day prednisone equivalent), which precipitate SRC.
- The card's Table 4 already lists captopril's indication as scleroderma renal crisis — **CONFIRMED**; it deserves a Table 2 row.

## E. HYPERTENSIVE ENCEPHALOPATHY
**Recommendation: merge with the PRES row rather than adding a separate one.** They are overlapping, not distinct — PRES is the radiographic correlate (vasogenic edema, usually parieto-occipital), hypertensive encephalopathy is the clinical syndrome and may have a normal MRI. Same target (MAP ↓20–25% over 1–2 h, expert consensus), same agents (nicardipine/clevidipine ± labetalol), same cautions (avoid nitroglycerin/nitroprusside where ICP is a concern). Hypertensive encephalopathy is a **diagnosis of exclusion** — rule out ischemic stroke, ICH, SAH first; reversal of the syndrome with BP control is confirmatory. The card's header already lists both under "End-organ damage: Neuro," so a merged row keeps them aligned.

## F. AUTONOMIC DYSREFLEXIA — **worth adding; the management is unlike every other row**
- **Who:** spinal cord injury **at or above T6**. The patient's baseline SBP is often 90–110, so **a "normal-looking" BP of 140 can be a 40-point emergency.** Trigger threshold = SBP ~20–40 mmHg above their baseline.
- **First actions are NOT pharmacologic:** **sit the patient upright**, lower the legs, loosen constrictive clothing/abdominal binder, then **find and remove the noxious stimulus** — almost always bladder (blocked catheter, distension) or bowel (impaction). Most episodes resolve with this alone.
- **Pharmacologic** only if measures fail and **SBP ≥150**: nitroglycerin ointment (1 inch, applied above the level of injury — **wipeable off** if hypotension develops), or **immediate-release nifedipine, bite-and-swallow (not sublingual)**; captopril also used.
- **Hard contraindication:** **nitrates if a PDE5 inhibitor was taken in the last 24–48 h** — very common in this population.
- **Citation:** Consortium for Spinal Cord Medicine CPG, "Evaluation and Management of Autonomic Dysreflexia and Other Autonomic Dysfunctions" — https://pva.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/CPG_Autonomic-Dysreflexia_2025_digital_03.26-.pdf

## G. ACUTE POST-TRANSPLANT HYPERTENSION — **do NOT add a row; footnote only**
No society guideline defines a BP target for a hypertensive emergency in a transplant recipient, so a row would be inventing numbers. Worth one footnote: the allograft is **denervated with impaired autoregulation**, so it may be injured at lower absolute pressures; **CCBs (nicardipine/clevidipine) are rational** because they oppose calcineurin-inhibitor-mediated afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction; watch the **CCB–CNI interaction** (diltiazem, verapamil and nicardipine inhibit CYP3A4 and raise tacrolimus/cyclosporine levels; amlodipine and isradipine are safer on that axis). CNI/mTOR combinations are a recognized cause of post-transplant TMA.

# OUT-OF-SCOPE BUT WORTH FLAGGING TO THE CARD AUTHOR

1. **Table 1 wording inversion.** Card: *"Reduce BP by max 25% within first hour, and to no lower than 160/100 within 2-6h."* The guideline says reduce by no more than 25% in the first hour, **then, if stable, TO <160/100** within the next 2–6 h. The card's "to no lower than 160/100" reads as a floor and inverts the intent. Suggested: *"...then to <160/100 over the next 2–6 h, then cautiously to 130–140 over 24–48 h."*
2. **Terminology.** The 2024 AHA statement uses "markedly elevated BP"; the **2025 AHA/ACC guideline uses "severe hypertension."** The card's "(formerly HTN urgency)" is right but could name both current terms.
3. **Table 3, nitroprusside "avoid in ... CVA."** The AIS guideline names nitroprusside as the fallback for refractory BP or DBP >140. The ICP-based caution is fine; the blanket "avoid" overstates it.
4. **Table 3, nitroglycerin max 400 mcg/min.** True for standard ADHF; understates the doses used in SCAPE.
